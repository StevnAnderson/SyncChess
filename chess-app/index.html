    How Sync Chess Works

    • Select a standard Chess legal move. Nothing will happen until your opponent also selects their move.
    • If both players attempt to move their pieces to the same empty square, both pieces are removed. (This can kill the king, ending the game.)
    • If two pieces try to capture each other, both pieces are captured and removed from the board. (This can kill a king, ending the game.)
    • Paths may be intercepted. If your piece ends its movement in the path of the opponent's moving piece, it will be taken, but it stops the opponent's piece in that square. (Pawns, if they move 2 squares on their first move, Bishops, Rooks, and Queens are the pieces that can be intercepted. They move along their path one square at a time as the opponent moves likewise. If you try to intercept from a far distance you may not make it there in time!)
    • Once a piece successfully captures another piece, it is "Delayed" for the next turn.
    • If a delayed piece is selected to move, it doesn't move until after the opponent's piece has finished moving, unless it is also delayed, then they both move simultaneously as normal.

    How Chess Works

    • Players alternate turns, moving one piece per turn, exactly like standard chess.
    • Castling, en passant, and pawn promotion all follow standard chess rules.
    • You may not leave your own king in check, so a king can never be captured.
    • Checkmate wins the game; stalemate is a draw.

    How Dark Chess Works

    • Dark Chess follows normal chess rules with one big difference: you only see the squares your own pieces can move to.
    • Every square you cannot move to is covered by fog of war. A piece might be there, or it might be empty.
    • There is no check and no checkmate. You are never warned that your king is attacked — if your king is captured, you lose.
    • Castling only requires the squares between the king and rook to be empty. You may castle while in check, through check, or into check.
    • En passant works normally, so the square behind an enemy pawn becomes visible while the capture is legal even if the pawn itself stays hidden.
    • In Pass & Play the board is hidden between turns, so each player only ever sees their own view.

    How Atomic Chess Works

    • Atomic follows normal chess rules, but every capture sets off an explosion.
    • The capturing piece and the captured piece are destroyed, along with every piece on the eight squares surrounding the captured square (pawns included).
    • You may not make a capture that would destroy your own king.
    • If an explosion destroys a king, that player loses immediately. If both kings are destroyed, the game is a draw.
    • Checkmate and stalemate still apply.

    How King of the Hill Chess Works

    • King of the Hill follows normal chess rules, with one extra way to win.
    • If your king moves onto any of the four center squares (d4, d5, e4, e5), you win immediately.
    • Checkmate and stalemate work exactly as they do in standard chess.

    How Three-Check Chess Works

    • Three-Check follows normal chess rules: checkmate still wins and stalemate is still a draw.
    • Every check you give is counted, and both counts are shown in the status bar.
    • The first player to give three checks wins the game immediately.

    How 4P Chess Works

    • Four players share a 14×14 board, one army in each corner. Play starts with Red and goes clockwise: Red, Blue, Yellow, Green.
    • Pawns may move two squares on their first move, capture diagonally, can be taken en passant, and promote automatically to a queen on the 8th rank from their starting side. An en passant chance stays open until the double-stepping pawn’s owner moves again, so every opponent gets one turn to take it. Castling follows normal chess rules.
    • Scoring: checkmate +20, self-stalemate +20, opponent stalemate +10 to each other player, and checks on two or more kings at once score bonus points.
    • Captures score 1 for a pawn or promoted pawn, 3 for a knight, 5 for a bishop or rook, and 9 for a queen. Capturing an eliminated player’s pieces scores nothing.
    • A King can be captured. In Sync mode, the player who captures the most Kings that round wins; equal king captures or all four Kings captured is a draw.
    • In Sync mode, if two moving pieces collide in transit, both are removed. If a piece intercepts another piece that has arrived on its path, the intercepted piece is taken, the mover stops, and the mover is Delayed next round. This collision can take a teammate’s piece.
    • In Sync mode, all active players lock in a move before pieces travel one square at a time. Capturing an active piece delays the capturer next round until non-delayed pieces finish moving; capturing a frozen or eliminated player’s piece does not cause a delay.
    • When a player is checkmated or stalemated, their pieces stay on the board but are frozen and can be captured for no points.
    • If a player resigns or runs out of time, their king stays alive and moves at random on its turns. Checkmating a zombie king scores 20 points; stalemating it gives 10 points to each remaining player.
    • When three players are eliminated, the highest score wins — you can win even if you were eliminated first.
    • With two players left, a player leading by 21 or more points may claim victory by resigning and awarding the other player 20 points. The leader still wins the game.

    How 4P Teams Works

    • Teams are Red and Yellow against Blue and Green. Teammates sit across the board from each other and cannot capture each other’s pieces.
    • Pawns promote on the 11th rank from their starting side, and underpromotion to a rook, bishop, or knight is allowed.
    • Checkmate one member of the opposing team to win. Stalemating a player draws the game.
    • Checkmate and stalemate are only judged when the affected player’s turn is reached, so another player can mate first to escape.
    • Castling follows normal chess rules, and pawns may move two squares on their first move. En passant works too: each opponent gets one turn to take a double-stepped pawn before its owner moves again.
    • In Sync mode, all active players lock in a move before pieces travel one square at a time. Capturing an active piece delays the capturer next round until non-delayed pieces finish moving; capturing a frozen player’s piece does not cause a delay.
    • A King can be captured. In Sync mode, the team that captures more Kings that round wins; equal king captures or all four Kings captured is a draw.
    • In Sync mode, if two moving pieces collide in transit, both are removed. If a piece intercepts another piece that has arrived on its path, the intercepted piece is taken, the mover stops, and the mover is Delayed next round. This collision can take a teammate’s piece.

    Time Control

    Set minutes and increment for each player. Set minutes to 0 for unlimited time. Increment is added after every completed move.
