    How Sync Chess Works

    • Select a standard Chess legal move. Nothing will happen until your opponent also selects their move.
    • If both players attempt to move their pieces to the same empty square, both pieces are removed. (This can kill the king, ending the game.)
    • If two pieces try to capture each other, both pieces are captured and removed from the board. (This can kill a king, ending the game.)
    • Paths may be intercepted. If your piece ends its movement in the path of the opponent's moving piece, it will be taken, but it stops the opponent's piece in that square. (Pawns, if they move 2 squares on their first move, Bishops, Rooks, and Queens are the pieces that can be intercepted. They move along their path one square at a time as the opponent moves likewise. If you try to intercept from a far distance you may not make it there in time!)
    • Once a piece successfully captures another piece, it is "Delayed" for the next turn.
    • If a delayed piece is selected to move, it doesn't move until after the opponent's piece has finished moving, unless it is also delayed, then they both move simultaneously as normal.

    How Chess Works

    • Players alternate turns, moving one piece per turn, exactly like standard chess.
    • Castling, en passant, and pawn promotion all follow standard chess rules.
    • You may not leave your own king in check, so a king can never be captured.
    • Checkmate wins the game; stalemate is a draw.

    How Dark Chess Works

    • Dark Chess follows normal chess rules with one big difference: you only see the squares your own pieces can move to.
    • Every square you cannot move to is covered by fog of war. A piece might be there, or it might be empty.
    • There is no check and no checkmate. You are never warned that your king is attacked — if your king is captured, you lose.
    • Castling only requires the squares between the king and rook to be empty. You may castle while in check, through check, or into check.
    • En passant works normally, so the square behind an enemy pawn becomes visible while the capture is legal even if the pawn itself stays hidden.
    • In Pass & Play the board is hidden between turns, so each player only ever sees their own view.
